## Executive Summary

- The global GLP-1 obesity market exceeded **$35 billion in combined 2024 revenues** across Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly, making it the fastest-growing pharmaceutical category in modern history.
- **Semaglutide (Wegovy/Ozempic)** and **tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro)** dominate commercially; tirzepatide is gaining share rapidly after its November 2023 obesity approval, driven by superior weight loss (~20–22% vs ~15% body weight).
- The **SELECT cardiovascular outcomes trial** (semaglutide, 2023) earned a first-of-its-kind CV risk reduction label for an obesity drug (March 2024 FDA approval), opening a potential Medicare Part D coverage pathway worth an estimated $5–10B in incremental annual revenue.
- The next-wave pipeline — **retatrutide** (~24% weight loss, Phase 3), **CagriSema** (~25% weight loss, Phase 3), and **orforglipron** (oral non-peptide, Phase 3) — threatens to re-rank market leadership by 2027–2028.

## 1. Market Overview & Sizing

**Current Revenue Base**

- **Ozempic** (semaglutide SC, T2D): ~$14.3B in 2024 global sales (significant off-label obesity use embedded)
- **Wegovy** (semaglutide 2.4 mg SC, obesity): ~$4.5–5B in 2024, supply-constrained
- **Mounjaro** (tirzepatide SC, T2D): ~$9.7B in 2024
- **Zepbound** (tirzepatide SC, obesity): ~$2.3B in first full year (2024)
- Combined GLP-1 franchise (both companies): **>$35B in 2024**

**Projections**

- Analyst consensus (Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Evaluate Pharma) projects the GLP-1 obesity/metabolic market at **$80–130B globally by 2030**, depending on oral penetration, Medicare access, and international rollout.
- US addressable obesity population: approximately **100 million adults** (BMI ≥30). Only ~3–5 million were on a GLP-1 agent as of mid-2025 — penetration below 5%.
- Global eligible population exceeds **750 million** with penetration in low single digits internationally.

**Key Market Drivers**

- SELECT trial CV outcome enabling Medicare and payer coverage expansion
- Multi-billion dollar manufacturing investment by both Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly
- Rapidly expanding HCP and patient awareness driving demand that outpaces supply
- Emerging clinical benefit data beyond weight loss: MASH/NASH, sleep apnea, CKD, HFpEF

## 4. Payer Access & Reimbursement

**US Commercial Coverage**

- Approximately **50–55% of commercially insured lives** had some GLP-1 obesity coverage as of early 2025, up from ~35% in 2023.
- Coverage typically requires prior authorization with BMI documentation, comorbidity confirmation, and dietary counseling attestation.
- List prices: Wegovy ~$1,350/month; Zepbound ~$1,060/month. Net prices estimated 30–50% lower after rebates.
- Several large self-insured employers have restricted or eliminated obesity drug coverage citing cost — a notable payer pushback dynamic creating market tension.

**Medicare Part D**

- The Inflation Reduction Act excluded obesity drugs from Medicare Part D unless they carry an indication beyond obesity.
- The **March 2024 CV risk reduction FDA label** for Wegovy (based on SELECT trial) created the regulatory basis for Medicare coverage; CMS is evaluating implementation.
- Full Medicare Part D coverage for Wegovy was not finalized as of mid-2025 but remains the single most impactful near-term revenue catalyst — estimated at **$5–10B in incremental annual revenue** for Novo Nordisk at scale.

**Compounded Semaglutide**

- FDA designated semaglutide in shortage status in 2023–2024, permitting compounding pharmacies to produce copies, significantly undercutting branded pricing.
- FDA moved to end shortage-based compounding in early 2025, triggering enforcement actions — a major commercial and access inflection point affecting hundreds of thousands of patients on compounded product.

**International Access**

- UK NICE approved Wegovy in 2023 with restrictive rollout (specialist setting only).
- EU markets (Germany, France) face reimbursement barriers; most uptake is out-of-pocket.
- Japan approved semaglutide (Ozempic) for obesity in 2023.

## 5. Regulatory Intelligence

**Key US FDA Actions**

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| March 2024 | Wegovy CV label expansion — first obesity drug approved to reduce serious CV events in adults with obesity/overweight + CVD |
| November 2023 | Zepbound (tirzepatide) FDA approval for obesity/overweight with comorbidity |
| June 2024 | Wegovy label expanded to adolescents aged 12+ |
| 2025 | Zepbound sNDA filed for obstructive sleep apnea (SURMOUNT-OSA positive Phase 3); FDA review underway |
| 2025 | Oral semaglutide (obesity dose) NDA under FDA review; PDUFA date mid-2025 |

**Regulatory Themes**

- FDA is establishing a pattern of approving **comorbidity-specific indications** (CV, sleep apnea, MASH) beyond weight loss alone, expanding labeling and payer coverage opportunities.
- No REMS currently required for GLP-1 obesity agents; FDA monitoring thyroid C-cell signals and pancreatitis, but benefit-risk profile remains clearly favorable.
- Compounding enforcement actions in 2025 are a direct regulatory lever reshaping the competitive landscape.
- EMA approved Wegovy in 2021; Mounjaro/Zepbound obesity indication under EMA review as of 2025.

## 7. Commercial Implications

**Scale of Opportunity**

- GLP-1 obesity is on trajectory to become the **largest pharmaceutical category in history** by 2029–2030, surpassing oncology leaders like pembrolizumab (~$25B).
- Every 1% increase in US treated obesity prevalence adds an estimated **$5–8B in annual market revenue** at current net pricing.

**Key Dynamics**

1. **Supply, not demand, is the binding constraint today.** Both companies are investing $10B+ in manufacturing expansion (2024–2026). The company that scales manufacturing fastest wins in the near term.
2. **Oral options are the next inflection point.** Orforglipron or oral semaglutide success could expand the treated population by 2–3x, particularly among needle-averse patients and those without specialty pharmacy access.
3. **Medicare CV coverage** is the single largest near-term binary catalyst — implementation would add millions of patients and billions in revenue, with tirzepatide following if it secures a comparable CV label.
4. **Payer dynamics will intensify.** As more agents compete, gross-to-net discounts will widen toward 50–60% by 2027–2028. Outcomes-based contracts are increasingly likely.
5. **Combination therapy** (GLP-1 + lean mass preservation, or GLP-1 + MASH agent) represents the premium tier of the next commercial wave.

**Strategic Recommendations by Stakeholder**

- **Novo Nordisk:** Monetize CV label aggressively for Medicare access; execute CagriSema Phase 3 to reclaim weight-loss leadership; convert oral semaglutide (obesity dose) into a new commercial growth vector.
- **Eli Lilly:** Position retatrutide as a premium weight-loss leader; deploy orforglipron as a market-expansion play targeting the untreated majority; build the bimagrumab combination story for 2027+.
- **New entrants (Roche, AstraZeneca, Pfizer, Amgen):** Differentiation is existential — a me-too subcutaneous GLP-1 entering in 2027+ has minimal commercial value. Oral, monthly dosing, or combination approaches are required for viability.
- **Payers and PBMs:** Outcomes-based contracts with CV event endpoints will become standard; companies with the richest real-world outcomes data hold negotiating leverage.
- **Commercial teams broadly:** Adherence and dropout rates remain commercially significant — patient support infrastructure, affordability programs, and HCP education on appropriate patient selection are critical to net revenue realization.

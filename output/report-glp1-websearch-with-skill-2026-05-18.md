## Executive Summary

- The GLP-1 receptor agonist market is the fastest-growing segment in pharmaceutical history, with 2024 combined revenues for Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly's obesity/diabetes franchises exceeding **$50B globally**; the obesity-specific market alone reached approximately **$13–14B** in 2024 and is projected to surpass **$130B by 2030**.
- Tirzepatide (Zepbound/Mounjaro) is rapidly eroding semaglutide's (Wegovy/Ozempic) dominant position on efficacy grounds — superior weight loss (~20–22% vs. ~15%) is driving formulary and consumer preference shifts; the duopoly will face credible oral and next-generation injectable challengers by 2026–2027.
- Payer access remains the single largest commercial bottleneck: fewer than 50% of commercially insured US lives have obesity-indicated GLP-1 coverage, Medicare Part D coverage for obesity indications remains structurally constrained despite the IRA, and employer plan dropout rates exceed 40% at 12 months.
- Strategic imperatives for commercial teams center on outcomes-data differentiation (cardiovascular, CKD, HFpEF, OSA), precision patient segmentation, and building durable adherence infrastructure — the companies that win market access and patient retention beyond the initial prescription will define the next five years of competitive advantage.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

### Approved Agents

**Semaglutide — Novo Nordisk**
- *Wegovy* (2.4mg SC weekly): FDA-approved June 2021; cardiovascular risk reduction label added March 2024. Mean weight loss: ~15% at 68 weeks (STEP 1).
- *Ozempic* (1mg/2mg SC weekly): T2D-approved; widely prescribed off-label for obesity.
- Novo Nordisk holds ~55–60% volume share in GLP-1 obesity, though declining under tirzepatide pressure.

**Tirzepatide — Eli Lilly**
- *Mounjaro* (2.5–15mg SC weekly): FDA-approved May 2022 for T2D — first GIP/GLP-1 dual agonist.
- *Zepbound* (15mg SC weekly): FDA-approved November 2023 for obesity. Mean weight loss: **~20.9%** at 72 weeks (SURMOUNT-1). By Q1 2025, Zepbound held approximately **35–40% new prescription share** in the obesity segment.

## 4. Payer Access & Reimbursement

### US Commercial Plan Coverage

- As of Q1 2025, approximately **45–50% of commercially insured US lives** have some GLP-1 obesity coverage.
- **PBM dynamics:** CVS/Caremark has positioned Zepbound as preferred over Wegovy on several formularies; Express Scripts and OptumRx maintain mixed or selective coverage with step therapy requirements.
- **Prior authorization burden:** Nearly all commercial plans require PA documenting BMI, comorbidities, and prior behavioral therapy attempts. Average approval cycle: 14–21 days; 20–30% initial denial rates.
- **Specialty tier OOP:** Patients typically face $150–300/month copays with coverage; Lilly and Novo Nordisk savings cards reduce cost to $0–50/month for eligible commercially insured patients.

### Medicare Part D / IRA Implications

- Medicare Part D is **statutorily prohibited** from covering weight loss drugs absent comorbidity-linked approval; the Treat and Reduce Obesity Act (TROA) has not passed as of 2025.
- Wegovy's CV risk reduction label (March 2024) created a coverage pathway for patients with established CVD + obesity — estimated 8–10 million Medicare beneficiaries. Approximately **30–40% of Part D plans** have activated this coverage as of Q1 2025.
- **Medicare Advantage:** ~15–20% of MA plan members have access to obesity GLP-1 coverage as a supplemental benefit.
- **IRA negotiation timeline:** GLP-1 agents will likely reach negotiation eligibility 2027–2028.

### Employer Plan Dynamics

- Large self-insured employers face **$6,000–12,000 per-member per-year** GLP-1 obesity costs; ~25–30% of large US employers (500+ employees) now offer obesity GLP-1 coverage (up from ~10% in 2022).
- Employers are coupling coverage with **mandatory obesity management program enrollment** to improve ROI.
- ~40–50% of employees discontinue within 12 months; innovative employers are piloting outcomes-based contracts with Novo Nordisk and Lilly.

## 7. Commercial Implications & Strategic Recommendations

### Launch Readiness Considerations

- The window for differentiated positioning on **weight loss efficacy alone is closing**. Retatrutide and CagriSema will surpass Wegovy efficacy; pure weight-loss claims will be commoditized by 2027.
- **Outcomes data** (CV, CKD, OSA, HFpEF, MASH) is the new currency of formulary access and prescriber confidence.
- **Manufacturing and supply reliability** is a hidden commercial variable: supply failures cost meaningful share.

### Patient Journey & Market Access Strategy

**Key friction points:**
1. **Diagnosis gap:** Only ~2% of eligible obese patients are actively prescribed an obesity medication; primary care recognition and initiation remain low.
2. **PA friction:** 14–21 day approval cycles; 20–30% initial denial rates.
3. **Adherence cliff at months 1–3:** GI side effects (nausea/vomiting in ~30–40% of patients) drive early discontinuation.
4. **Refill abandonment:** Savings card programs reduce initiation barriers, but specialty tier OOP drives abandonment in months 3–6.

### 3 Actionable Strategic Recommendations

**Recommendation 1: Anchor All Payer Strategy to Cardiovascular and Comorbidity Outcomes**
The SELECT trial demonstrated that outcomes data unlocks Medicare and commercial coverage in ways weight loss data alone cannot. Commercial teams should proactively engage pharmacy directors with outcomes value dossiers quantifying hospitalization avoidance, CV event reduction, and CKD progression delay — converting GLP-1 from a discretionary lifestyle budget line to a cardiovascular therapeutics investment.

**Recommendation 2: Build a Precision Patient Segmentation Model Organized Around Comorbidity**
Four sub-populations warrant distinct commercial strategies:
- *CVD + obesity*: Target cardiologists and preventive CV clinics with SELECT data
- *T2D + obesity*: Dual-indication positioning; tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism resonates with endocrinologists
- *OSA + obesity*: Zepbound's OSA label opens a novel sleep medicine prescriber channel — largely untapped
- *Obesity-only, commercially insured*: DTC and primary care activation; oral formulations will dominate here by 2027

**Recommendation 3: Invest in Retention Infrastructure as a Core Commercial Asset**
With 50–60% discontinuation at 12 months, every patient who discontinues represents lost revenue and eroded payer confidence. Commercial teams should: (a) partner with digital health vendors for integrated coaching programs; (b) structure outcomes-based rebates that reward plans for supporting adherence infrastructure; (c) build patient support programs with GI side effect management protocols and 90-day refill outreach.

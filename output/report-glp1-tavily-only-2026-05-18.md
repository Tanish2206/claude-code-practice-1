## 2. Key Players and Competitive Dynamics

**Novo Nordisk — Market Leader (Under Pressure)**
- Dominant with semaglutide franchise across T2D (Ozempic), obesity (Wegovy), and cardiovascular (SELECT trial: **20% reduction in MACE** in non-diabetic obese patients)
- Share price significantly corrected in late 2024–2025 following Lilly pipeline readouts
- Committed **$6+ billion** in U.S. manufacturing expansion
- Oral semaglutide (Rybelsus) in T2D; higher-dose oral obesity formulation in Phase 3

**Eli Lilly — Rapidly Closing Gap**
- Tirzepatide (dual GIP/GLP-1) demonstrating superior weight loss vs. semaglutide (SURMOUNT trials: ~**22% body weight reduction**)
- Retatrutide (triple GLP-1/GIP/glucagon) in Phase 3 — Phase 2 showed **~24% weight loss**
- Orforglipron (oral small molecule GLP-1) in Phase 3
- Manufacturing investment of **$9+ billion** in U.S. facilities
- Zepbound approved for **obstructive sleep apnea** in December 2024 — first pharmacological treatment for OSA, major label expansion

**Roche / Genentech**
- Acquired Carmot Therapeutics in **January 2024 for ~$3.1 billion** — added CT-388 (dual GLP-1/GIP), CT-996 (oral GLP-1), CT-868
- CT-388 Phase 2 showing ~19% weight loss at 24 weeks
- Positioned as serious third-wave competitor by 2027–2028

**Pfizer**
- Oral GLP-1 candidate **danuglipron** deprioritized in 2024 after Phase 2b liver enzyme elevation signals
- Relatively weakened competitive position vs. 2023 expectations; may pursue M&A to rebuild pipeline

**AstraZeneca**
- **ECC5004** (oral GLP-1, with Eccogene) — Phase 2 data expected 2025–2026
- **Petrelintide (AZD6234)** amylin analog for combination approaches
- Strategy: combination therapies pairing GLP-1 with amylin and GIPR agonists

**Amgen**
- **Maritide (AMG 133)** — monthly injectable GLP-1/GIP agonist-antagonist; Phase 3 MARITIME program fully enrolled
- Phase 2 showed ~14–15% weight loss at 52 weeks; differentiated by **monthly dosing** vs. weekly standard
- Phase 3 readout expected 2026

**Emerging Challengers**
- **Viking Therapeutics**: VK2735 (subcutaneous and oral) — Phase 2 oral data ~14% WL; fast-follower positioning; multiple large pharma reportedly in acquisition discussions
- **Zealand Pharma / Boehringer Ingelheim**: survodutide (GLP-1/glucagon) in Phase 3 for NASH and obesity
- **Structure Therapeutics**: GSBR-1290 oral GLP-1 — positive Phase 2

## 4. Payer Coverage and Access Barriers

**U.S. Coverage Landscape**
- **Medicare**: Wegovy approved for Medicare Part D coverage for cardiovascular risk reduction (post-SELECT trial) — limited to CV-indicated patients; obesity-only coverage still not universal
- **Commercial**: Approximately **50–55% of commercially insured lives** have some GLP-1 obesity coverage, though prior authorization requirements are extensive
- **Employer plans**: Large self-insured employers increasingly adding coverage; many capping annual benefit at $5,000–$10,000 or limiting by BMI thresholds

**Pricing Dynamics**
- **Wegovy WAC**: approximately **$1,349/month** in the U.S.; net price after rebates estimated $600–900/month
- **Zepbound WAC**: approximately **$1,060/month** — Lilly priced below Wegovy to gain formulary position
- **Out-of-pocket burden**: Even with insurance, patient cost-sharing often $200–$500+/month — significant adherence barrier
- **Compounding pharmacies**: FDA ended Wegovy/Zepbound shortage designations in 2024; enforcement against compounders ongoing into 2025–2026

**International Access**
- Europe: National health systems have very limited obesity reimbursement; Wegovy approved in EU but coverage fragmented
- U.K. NHS: Wegovy pilot programs through specialist clinics; highly rationed access
- Emerging markets: Minimal formal payer coverage; primarily out-of-pocket

**Adherence and Discontinuation**
- Real-world data showing **~50% of patients discontinue** GLP-1 therapy within 12 months, primarily due to cost and GI side effects
- Discontinuation leads to weight regain — raising durability questions for payers
- "Obesity as chronic disease" narrative being advanced by Novo Nordisk and Lilly to justify continuous coverage

## 6. Commercial Opportunity and Market Trends

**Macro Opportunity**
- **~650 million obese adults globally**; ~110 million in the U.S. alone
- Despite massive sales, only **3–5% of eligible obese patients** in the U.S. are on GLP-1 therapy — enormous untapped market

**Key Commercial Trends**

1. **Indication expansion as growth driver**: Every new approved indication (OSA, CV, CKD, MASH) unlocks new payer populations and justifies premium pricing
2. **Oral formulations as market expander**: Oral GLP-1s will dramatically lower barriers to initiation — potential to 5–10x the addressable patient pool if priced accessibly
3. **Combination therapies**: Pairing GLP-1 with amylin (cagrilintide, petrelintide) or anti-myostatin agents could achieve 25–30%+ weight loss — premium tier product opportunity
4. **Manufacturing as strategic moat**: Supply constraints have been a binding competitive factor; companies controlling manufacturing scale have pricing and access advantages
5. **Direct-to-patient platforms**: Novo Care (Novo Nordisk), LillyDirect (Lilly) launching direct-to-patient models — disintermediating traditional pharmacy/PBM channel
6. **Real-world evidence race**: Companies investing heavily in post-marketing studies across cardiometabolic, renal, and neurodegenerative endpoints to expand payer access and labeling
7. **Biosimilar horizon**: Semaglutide U.S. patent cliff expected ~2031–2032; tirzepatide later; biosimilar competition will reshape pricing and access dynamics at decade's end
8. **MASH expansion**: GLP-1s plus NASH-specific agents; Novo Nordisk semaglutide MASH data positive; major indication expansion underway
9. **Beyond obesity signaling**: Exploratory data on addiction reduction (alcohol, opioids), Alzheimer's — each signal a potential long-term pipeline catalyst
10. **Global expansion**: Japan, China, Middle East, Brazil — significant growth as regulatory approvals and local manufacturing partnerships form
